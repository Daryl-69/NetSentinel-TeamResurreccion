The Sentry Decision

Four shapes for the Inspector–Sentry cascade. The original reason for the cascade — GPU cost — does not survive measurement. These are the options that remain, and the evidence for each.

What every option shares

The sensor and feature pipeline are identical in all four. Nothing below this line is being decided.

The finding that reopened this

At 100,000 hosts with a 200-million-parameter teacher — a thousand times larger than the current one — inspecting every host-hour for a year costs $150. The cascade saves $135 of it. The cost argument for putting a cheap model first is arithmetically real and economically irrelevant at any scale below roughly ten million hosts.

Separately: on LANL, six methods spanning a 205k-parameter graph transformer down to a median-and-MAD all score between 0.54 and 0.58 within-host. Accuracy does not discriminate between these options either.

The four options

Same four nodes in every diagram, in the same positions. What changes between them is which edges exist and where the boundary falls.

A Cascade as built Justification broken

Why it was chosen

To avoid running a 205k-parameter graph model on every host-hour. That premise has now been measured and it does not hold.

What breaks under scrutiny

  • Cost saving is $135/year at 100k hosts
  • Budget counted in windows, load incurred in host-days — a 9.8× gap
  • Alert volume falls only 3.1%; the router is faithful by design
B Inspector only — drop the router Simplest, loses two properties

What it gains

  • One model, one threshold, no distillation, no routing loss
  • Nothing to explain about budgets or fidelity
  • Recovers the 3.1% of teacher flags the router drops

What it costs

  • Latency. The Inspector consumes a complete 24-window day. Nothing is scored until midnight.
  • Placement. It needs hop-2 cohort — other hosts' traffic in the same window — so it cannot run at a single site behind a diode.
C Two-speed split — edge and centre Recommended shape

Why this is the defensible one

  • Latency is structural, not a claim. The Inspector is a Transformer over all 24 windows; the Sentry is a unidirectional GRU that scores after each hour.
  • Placement is structural too. Cohort context does not exist at a single diode-separated site. The Sentry does not need it.
  • Both arguments survive a reviewer with a calculator. The cost argument does not.

What you must not say

  • Do not carry 8.6× onto a GPU slide. It is CPU-to-CPU. The Inspector's Transformer parallelises across all 24 windows; the Sentry's GRU is 24 sequential steps a GPU cannot parallelise. On GPU the gap shrinks and may invert.
  • That is precisely why the Sentry belongs on CPU — present it as a placement decision, backed by a benchmark you have run.
D Baseline-primary — the measured best detector leads Best accuracy, weakest story

The case for it

  • Highest measured within-host AUC of anything tested: 0.578
  • No training, no distillation, no GPU, trivially explainable to an auditor
  • Honest: it is what the numbers say

The case against

  • 0.578 is still near chance — this is not "the baseline works", it is "nothing works on LANL"
  • Discards the one thing that can use cross-service structure before you have tested it on a corpus that has cross-service structure
  • Ensembling it with the Inspector was tested and beat neither parent

Inspector within-host AUC is 0.542 for the single checkpoint all six methods were compared against; the 3-seed mean is 0.557 ± 0.013. The bag baseline's 0.578 carries a per-host spread of ± 0.273 across 77 hosts. Neither is meaningfully above chance.

What to do

Take Option C's shape. It keeps both models, changes nothing you have already built, and replaces a justification that fails arithmetic with two that are structural: the Inspector cannot emit before midnight, and it cannot run where cross-host context does not exist. Neither depends on a price list.

Leave the teacher undecided. A, B, C and D all score the same on LANL because everything scores the same on LANL — six methods between 0.54 and 0.58. That corpus is de-identified, all-internal, and substitutes a port-derived taxonomy; it cannot test a hypothesis about named service combinations. Choosing the teacher on its evidence is choosing on noise.

Decide it on 17 September, when the snaplen-0 capture has five days — the only corpus that has ever contained the signal this architecture was built for. Run the same three tests already scripted: the Inspector, the bag baseline, and the paired sign test between them. Whichever wins there is the teacher, and the answer is publishable either way.
